## Summary
The paper proposes a Lean4-mechanized formal model (UAD/f) defining two dual operators — U0 (join/preimage union, root coverage baseline) and U∧ (meet, simultaneous satisfiability) — for multi-layer specification governance. A PoC (n=3 OSS projects, interval constraints only) demonstrates deterministic replay of a regex-based extraction pipeline with source locking. The manuscript is unusually careful: it enumerates Non-goals explicitly, restricts statistical claims to feasibility demonstration, and accurately characterizes the n=3 convenience sample. Four required fixes remain before publication is safe. None require new proofs or experiments; all are textual/structural. The most important (R2) is that §4.3's conditional operational corollaries—which depend on hSound/hComplete holding for the actual extractor—are not sufficiently demarcated from the PoC context where those conditions are unverified, creating a risk that readers interpret the corollaries as applying to the regex extractor.

## Strengths
- Systematic Non-goals enumeration (§0.3) prevents most over-claim problems: no statistical generalization from n=3, no extractor soundness/completeness proof, no deployment guarantee — all explicitly listed.
- Lean4 mechanization surfaces implicit assumptions as verifiable proof obligations. The no_left_adjoint_of_partial theorem and the hproj same-point connectivity condition are non-trivial design decisions that would have remained hidden without mechanization.
- Must/may semantics separation (§4.7) with explicit three-value judgement (consistent/contradictory/inconclusive) and policy-projection table provides a practically actionable framework without conflating the formal model and operational policy.
- Deterministic replay with SHA256-locked snapshots, reproduce.sh, and Python-stdlib-only dependencies makes RQ6 independently verifiable — exemplary reproducibility practice for the field.
- One-sided adequacy decomposition (§4.3: sound-only vs. complete-only inclusions) correctly avoids the ↔ strong assumption and stages the connection to concrete extractors, which is theoretically sound.
- The §4.8 theorem-applicability table (listing which theorems are verified vs. unverified in PoC context) is an honest and useful artifact that reviewers rarely see.

## Required Fixes
- R1 (Terminology inconsistency — join/meet vs. specification strength): The paper fixes the lattice order as ⊆ in §2.3 and correctly states U0=∪ is weaker (more permissive) under that order. However, in prose elsewhere U0 is described as a 'unified' or 'integrated' specification, which readers from type-theory or lattice-theory backgrounds may read as 'stronger.' A single canonical boxed remark must be inserted at the first substantive use of U0 in §3.1 (or §0.2): 'Under the inclusion order ⊆, U0 = ∪ lifted(i) is the least upper bound (join) and therefore the weakest (most permissive) combined constraint — it is an over-approximation envelope, not a tightened specification.' Without this, the join/meet directionality is a persistent reader-misinterpretation risk.
- R2 (Publication-safety risk — conditional operational corollaries in §4.3 lack adequate demarcation from PoC context): §4.3 correctly states that adequacy theorems are for abstract relation E and applying them to a concrete extractor requires separately proving hSound/hComplete. However, the '運用上の帰結' (operational corollaries) block in §4.3 is presented without a clear warning that these corollaries do NOT apply to the PoC regex extractor in §6, because that extractor's hSound/hComplete are not proven. A reader reading §4.3 then §6 can conclude that the corollaries (e.g., 'sound side suppresses false consistency reports') apply to the PoC. Insert a clearly delimited WARNING block immediately before the 運用上の帰結 subsection: 'The following corollaries are conditional on hSound/hComplete holding for the extractor in use. These properties are NOT verified for the regex extractor in §6 (see §0.3 Non-goals and §4.8). Applying these corollaries to §6 results requires separate extractor proof obligations.'
- R3 (Understated gap between RQ2 contribution claim and trivial D(i) in all experiments): §0.2 frames RQ2 as 'can A(i) ⊆ D(i) be lifted to root witness validity?' implying this is a demonstrated contribution. §2.1 provides extensive motivation for D(i)/A(i) separation. Yet §6.2 discloses that D(i) = ℤ×ℤ (trivial) throughout all PoC experiments, and the Non-goals list in §0.3 does not mention this gap. RQ2 is answered only at the theory/Lean level; the D(i) separation is never exercised concretely. Add to §0.3 Non-goals: 'D(i) separation is modeled and type-checked in Lean but D(i) is set to the trivial domain (ℤ×ℤ) in all PoC experiments; empirical validation of domain-constrained D(i) (unit normalization, type-domain constraints) is deferred to future work.' This prevents readers from overinterpreting RQ2 as empirically demonstrated.
- R4 (Ambiguous mutation detection in table — heterogeneous detection criteria presented uniformly): The §6.2 mutation table shows expectation_satisfied=true for all 6 mutations, but the detection criteria are heterogeneous: stale_requirement_lower mutations are detected by raw_judgement==contradictory (U∧ detection), while unit_mismatch_upper_scale_down_1024 mutations are detected by upper' <= floor(upper/1024) (bound shrinkage criterion, not U∧ contradiction). The zlib unit_mismatch row has mutated_intersection=[-1,0] which is a valid interval (consistent, not contradictory), yet is reported as 'detected.' The claim mutation_detected_by_expectation=6 (out of 6) is technically correct but conflates two qualitatively different detection mechanisms. Add a 'detection_method' column to the table with values 'U∧ contradiction' or 'bound_shrinkage_criterion', and add a footnote: 'Not all detections indicate U∧ inconsistency; unit_mismatch detections use a bound-shrinkage criterion that may be satisfied even when raw_judgement=consistent.'

## Optional Fixes
- O1: §3.4 MUS definition uses ⊊ notation inconsistently with the ⊆ notation used throughout the paper. Standardize to a single notation convention (e.g., define proper subset explicitly using ⊆ and ≠, or use ⊊ everywhere it applies) to avoid typographical confusion.
- O2: The §4.8 theorem-applicability table (which theorems are PoC-verified vs. unverified) is very useful but appears after the adequacy section. Add a forward reference from §4.3 immediately after the adequacy theorems pointing to §4.8: 'See §4.8 for which of these theorems are verified vs. unverified in the PoC context.' This reduces the distance between the adequacy claim and its PoC qualification.
- O3: §7.2 reproducibility section lists SHA256 hashes tied to 2026-02-15. Add an explicit warning at the top of §7.2: 'Online mode (without --offline-lock) fetches live documents and may produce different results if documents change. Always use offline mode with the bundled snapshots for exact reproduction of reported results.'
- O4: The term '根被覆基準' (root coverage baseline) appears in the title and §0.1 but is not formally defined until §3.1. Add a one-line parenthetical definition at first use in §0.1: 'root coverage baseline (根被覆基準): the preimage union ∪ proj_i⁻¹(A(i)) that covers all layer-admissible root states.'
- O5: §6.1.1 proves goodBundle ∈ U0 and goodBundle ∈ UAnd in Lean for a specific closed instance (ArtifactBundle). Add a parenthetical clarifying this is an instance proof, not a general result, to prevent readers from inferring that the Lean proof establishes universal validity: 'This is a machine-checked proof for the specific ArtifactBundle instance defined in the file, not a general guarantee across all artifact configurations.'
